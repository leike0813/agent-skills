## Evidence discipline

Treat AI-like style as a cluster-detection problem. Search terms locate candidates; they do not establish findings.

Use three kinds of evidence:

- **Local:** the quoted span visibly performs the named mechanism, such as repeating one proposition in new words.
- **Distributional:** the construction recurs across a bounded region, such as six paragraphs with the same closing move.
- **Contextual:** the construction has no needed genre, disciplinary, structural, or rhetorical function.

Strong findings usually combine local evidence with either repetition or context. An isolated weak cue belongs in unresolved observations or nowhere.

Confidence:

- `high`: mechanism visible, repeated or clustered, and no plausible contextual explanation remains;
- `medium`: mechanism visible but could partly arise from genre, translation, or required structure;
- `low`: cue isolated, coverage partial, or context absent.

Severity:

- `high`: distorts claim strength, hides agency, repeats substantial content, or dominates a section;
- `medium`: repeatedly pads reasoning, flattens movement, or makes relations harder to follow;
- `low`: local and mainly stylistic.

Keep confidence and severity separate.

## Information-unit test

Before recommending deletion, merge, or split, write a compact proposition for each sentence or clause. Mark anything that changes:

- the asserted event or relation;
- the evidence or example;
- population, time, condition, comparison, or exception;
- certainty, negation, limitation, or causal strength;
- the logical link to nearby claims;
- terminology needed for stable reference;
- the paragraph's rhetorical job.

Two sentences are redundant only when these marks are the same and the repetition has no purposeful emphasis, recap, or cohesion function. A shorter rewrite is unsafe when it drops even one marked difference.

## Content probes

### Significance and promotion

Ask:

- Does the passage state importance without evidence or attribution?
- Could the significance clause be removed without changing the factual claim?
- Does an evaluative adjective replace a measurable property?
- Does a trailing participial phrase merely rename the preceding fact as evidence of success, resilience, or importance?

Safe change: state the concrete fact or supported evaluation directly.

Unsafe change: removing an explicit authorial interpretation that the surrounding argument develops, or weakening a sourced significance judgment.

### Attribution and unsupported filling

Ask:

- Can the named source be identified from the text?
- Does `research suggests` summarize cited evidence or hide that no evidence is supplied?
- Does a gap disclaimer turn into guesses about motives, biography, reception, or future behavior?

Never invent a source. A bounded suggestion may be “name the existing source” or “remove the attribution frame if this is the author's claim.”

### Challenges and endings

Compare the ending with the preceding evidence. Generic resilience, bright-future, and ongoing-journey language carries no new unit. A conclusion that synthesizes actual findings, implications, limitations, or next steps remains functional even when it repeats key terms.

### Author stance and sterile neutrality

First identify what the genre permits and what the source already claims. A methods section may appropriately suppress the actor; a discussion or argumentative essay may need the author's stated interpretation to remain visible.

Flag stance erasure only when local or document-level evidence shows that an existing judgment has been converted into anonymous, uniformly cautious prose. The absence of `I` or `we` alone is not evidence. A safe intervention can restore an actor, judgment verb, or evidence-matched assertion already supported by the source or explicit user input. If the needed view, disagreement, limitation, surprise, or cognitive history is absent, record the item as unresolved and request author input. Never manufacture it as a humanizing device.

## Lexical and syntactic probes

### Vocabulary clusters

For each watched word, identify its job. `Landscape` may be the exact geographic subject; `significant` may report a statistical test; `foster` may be a quoted policy term. Flag a cluster only when prestige vocabulary substitutes for specific relations or recurs independent of topic.

### Copula avoidance and nominalization

Try a plain predicate mentally:

- `serves as an illustration of` → `shows`;
- `conducted an examination of` → `examined`;
- `the implementation of X resulted in an improvement in Y` → `X improved Y`.

Keep the original when the nominalization names a stable method, construct, legal act, or object of analysis. Restoring an actor is safe only when the source identifies that actor.

### Participial tails

Test whether the `-ing` phrase supplies a supported simultaneous action, means, or consequence. If it only praises or vaguely interprets the main clause, remove it. Do not turn correlation into causation while making the sentence direct.

### Hedging and boosting

List the proposition and its evidence level. Delete stacked duplicates, but retain qualifiers that encode epistemic status. `May` and `is associated with` can carry different causal commitments; do not compress them blindly.

### Chinese and cross-language prose

Check whether an opening or conclusion can be removed without losing topic, scope, or relation. Common candidates include `In recent years`, `with the rapid development of`, `在……背景下`, `随着……不断发展`, generic importance claims, abstract-noun chains, and repeated `从而/进而/此外/因此` sequences.

A background sentence is actionable only when it adds no bounded time, mechanism, source, or study scope and merely delays a concrete claim already present. Begin with that existing claim; do not invent a trend, citation, or historical frame to replace the ritual opening.

Do not flag:

- terminology established in the field;
- conventional thesis language required by an institution;
- structures created by accurate translation of a source concept;
- four-character phrases that are ordinary, precise, or part of the author's voice.

When suggesting a direct Chinese rewrite, retain logical particles that encode actual contrast, cause, concession, or progression.

## Organization probes

### Connector density

Create a short list of paragraph-initial and sentence-initial connectors. Ask what relation each one adds. A connector is expendable when adjacency already makes the relation obvious or when it announces organization without expressing contrast, cause, sequence, or scope.

Do not remove transitions until paragraphs become a pile of unsupported claims. Logical visibility is part of information preservation.

### Paragraph templates

Represent each paragraph by function: topic, definition, evidence, interpretation, limitation, transition, synthesis. Repetition becomes suspicious when unrelated paragraphs share the same movement and closing sentence without a genre requirement.

Intervene locally. Changing one empty closing sentence may be enough; do not reorganize sections to create variety.

### Lists and headings

Ask whether readers need to scan, compare, follow a procedure, or refer to named items. If yes, preserve the list. Inline-header bullets are weak evidence when the labels form a genuine taxonomy or interface contract.

Headings need not vary for their own sake. Preserve prescribed capitalization and hierarchy.

## Rhythm probes

Sentence length is only one part of rhythm. For a statistical uniform run, inspect:

- repeated sentence openings or grammatical subjects;
- identical clause counts;
- repeated transition-plus-claim shapes;
- the same punctuation at the same relative position;
- repeated manufactured closers;
- whether the run is a protocol, list, abstract, definition block, or deliberate cadence.

A supported F37 finding requires both the run and a mechanical local pattern. A natural intervention uses an existing semantic boundary to split or merges propositions that already belong together. Never add filler to lengthen a sentence or delete information to shorten it.

One short sentence is normal. Several short, abstract declarations that manufacture drama support pattern 31. One em dash is typography; repeated dashes that replace varied clause relations can support pattern 14.

## Voice-preservation probes

Before changing a marked feature, look for evidence that it belongs to the author:

- the same punctuation or opener recurs in purposeful contexts;
- an aside reveals genuine uncertainty or self-correction;
- first-person stance has a defensible role;
- odd detail, dated reference, or mixed feeling resists generic smoothing;
- lexical complexity matches the discipline and surrounding paragraphs;
- repetition maintains a deliberate motif or term.

Use only evidence visible in the source itself when deciding whether a marked feature belongs to the author.

## Selecting an intervention

Choose the smallest safe operation:

| Mechanism | Usually safe | Common risk |
|---|---|---|
| Exact semantic duplicate | Delete or merge the duplicate span | Dropping a qualification hidden in the second version |
| Unsupported significance | Remove evaluative frame | Removing the author's explicit interpretation |
| Vague attribution | Name an existing source or state the claim directly | Inventing attribution or overstating ownership |
| Copula avoidance | Restore `is`, `are`, or `has` | Flattening a predicate with real semantic content |
| Nominalization chain | Restore a precise verb | Losing a technical construct |
| Generic connector | Delete or replace with the actual relation | Making argument order unclear |
| Paragraph template | Remove the empty move | Restructuring the document beyond scope |
| Uniform rhythm | Split/merge at a semantic boundary | Altering emphasis or chasing statistics |
| Ritual Chinese framing | Begin with the concrete scope or claim | Removing required institutional phrasing |
| Erased author stance | Restore a source-backed actor or evidence-matched judgment | Inventing an opinion, disagreement, or limitation |

When several approved findings depend on one another, plan them in this order: remove zero-information repetition; remove unsupported evaluation and ritual phrasing; restore precise verbs; repair empty connectors and metadiscourse; resolve paragraph or list symmetry; adjust sentence rhythm at semantic boundaries; restore source-backed stance and hedge strength; then rescan language-specific patterns and regressions. Skip any class without a supported finding. This dependency order prevents polishing text that a prior safe deletion will remove and does not authorize broader restructuring.

Suggestions in review mode should name the operation and preservation constraint. Finished replacement wording is optional and should be short enough to demonstrate feasibility, not an unapproved rewrite of the document.

## Worked examples

### Repetition with a hidden qualification

> The intervention reduced response time in the treatment group. Participants who completed all six sessions responded faster, although the difference was not observed among early dropouts.

The second sentence is not a duplicate. It narrows the population and adds an exception. Deleting it would violate information preservation.

### Inflated tail

> The archive contains 1,842 letters, underscoring its crucial role as an enduring testament to local memory.

The count is concrete; the rest stacks unsupported significance. A safe suggestion is to retain the count and delete the evaluative tail, unless the document later argues for that cultural role.

### Functional passive voice

> Samples were heated to 80 °C for ten minutes and then stored at 4 °C.

The procedure, not the operator, is the focus. The passive is conventional and precise here. Do not flag it without another mechanism.

### Statistical run with a genre explanation

> Add 2 mL buffer. Mix for 30 seconds. Incubate for five minutes. Record the absorbance.

Similar sentence lengths arise from imperative protocol steps. The run is not an F37 finding.

### Chinese ritual framing

> 随着数字技术的不断发展，数字平台在社会生活中发挥着越来越重要的作用。

This is suspicious only if it supplies no bounded time, technology, mechanism, or supported importance claim. If the next sentence already states the actual platform change, suggest beginning there. If the sentence defines the study's historical scope, preserve that scope in any edit.

## Final challenge before reporting

For every retained finding, answer:

1. What exact mechanism is visible?
2. What contextual alternative did I rule out?
3. What information must survive the suggested operation?
4. Could the same root cause cover another affected span?
5. Would a careful human editor plausibly leave this unchanged?
6. If the finding concerns voice, what source or user evidence authorizes the proposed stance?

If the fifth answer is yes because of genre, voice, or uncertainty, lower confidence or move the item to unresolved observations.
